What does a custom kitchen project in Riga involve?

The capital of Latvia on the banks of the Daugava with many different neighborhoods and very different residential buildings.

In Riga, at the beginning of the project, it is especially important to separate space planning from delivery logistics. An apartment in a historic center building, a kitchen in a typical multi-apartment building and a room in a private house require a different order of measurement, module division and assembly.

Address and delivery access

In an apartment building, check the stairwell, elevator, doorways, floor and the possibility of temporarily placing the supplied modules in time. In an older building, do not assume that the walls, floor and corners are geometrically straight.

What to check at a property in Riga

  • Specify neighborhood, building type, floor and elevator presence;a street name alone is not enough for a delivery plan.
  • In a historic house, fix wall and floor deviations, anchorage foundation and narrowest points of staircase.
  • In a typical apartment, check the ventilation, existing connections, windowsills and full openings for appliances.
  • Add gate, entrance and patio door information to the private house, as well as the connection of the kitchen to the living area.

Decision Sequence

What should be decided first in the Riga project

In a historical house in Riga, a standardized division of cabinets may not work due to deviations in the walls, but in a typical apartment, the ventilation unit may be critical. The type of building determines the first technical inspection.

The final front colour can be refined later, while incorrect module sizes or appliance openings are difficult to correct. Therefore, the sequence of decisions starts with geometry and function.

Local Risk Check

Riga neighborhood is not a sufficient description of delivery

A historic building without an elevator, a typical apartment building and a private house can be located in one neighborhood. That's why the address is just the beginning: we collect the building type, floor, access and the actual limitations of the space before the project and delivery.

  • In a historic building, we check uneven walls, floor levels and fixing surfaces rather than making assumptions based on the building's age.
  • In an apartment building, we compare the dimensions of modules with elevators, stairwells, entrance doors and corridor turns.
  • In a private house, we evaluate the gate, entrance, terrace door and visibility of the kitchen from the common living space.

In the Riga project, a precise description of the building and access reduces confusion between the idea of ​​the space and its implementation. We use it as a checklist in measurements and planning discussions, especially in areas with complex shared paths.
