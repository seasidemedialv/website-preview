Ideas and choices

Kitchen design from idea to thoughtful solution

A good kitchen design combines aesthetics with everyday use. This guide helps organize visual choices to support layout, storage, light and the overall character of the space.

Colors and materialsProportions and lightConnection to layout

Where to start choosing a kitchen design?

The starting point is not one fashionable color or a picture saved on social networks. First you need to name the nature of the room, the daily habits and the features you want to preserve. A spacious, bright kitchen and a compact room with limited daylight may require different shades, textures and density of details. Also, an open-plan kitchen should be seen together with the living area, not as a separate set of furniture.

Choose two or three words for the mood you want, such as calm, warm and concise. They help to compare options and to reject choices that seem beautiful individually, but do not create a unified result together. Then check that the design matches the functional plan and that there is an understandable reason for each visible line.

Function sets the foundation for composition

Rhythm of fronts derives from modules, equipment slots, and storage. If the visual division is made before these decisions, uneven lines or accidentally narrow elements may appear later. Therefore, first determine the working areas, openings and height relationships, and then choose which verticals to highlight and which to visually calm.

High cabinets can form a monolithic background, while the work area - a lighter, horizontal composition. In a small kitchen, the number of details should often be limited so that the space does not seem fragmented. In a larger room, an empty plane is as important as the amount of storage: not every wall should be filled to the last centimeter.

Fast design check

  • Do the colors match the light in the room?
  • Do the facade lines follow the modules?
  • Are the materials suitable for the particular load?
  • Does the kitchen fit into the adjoining rooms?

Material palette

How to coordinate colors, textures and work surface

A uniform palette does not mean that all surfaces must be the same. This means that each material has its own role and their mutual relations are conscious.

Base tone

The largest plane of fronts sets the overall mood. Evaluate the sample in the room at different times of the day, as warm and cool light can show the same shade differently.

Worktop

It is both a visual and intensively used surface. Choose the pattern, thickness and edge together with fronts, wall decoration and maintenance habits.

Accents

Wood texture, handles or a single contrasting volume can add depth. Multiple competing accents in turn create visual noise.

Light and perception

The design should be evaluated in real light

Daylight direction, window size and shade of artificial lighting affect both color and texture. A matte surface diffuses glare, while a glossy surface can amplify light and reveal more reflections. Dark tones are not automatically unsuitable for a small room, but they do require a thoughtful balance of light and contrast.

Separate general room light, work surface light and mood light. A decorative lamp can become the accent of the composition, but it does not replace uniform lighting in the place where cooking is done. The lighting design must be coordinated with the final plan before production and with the relevant specialists.

Images are for inspiration, not for copying

Kitchen images used on the website are illustrative. When saving a reference, write down exactly what you like: color ratio, division of fronts, material texture or light. This is more valuable than trying to replicate an alien space.

Typical mistakes

Which weakens even a good visual idea

Too many samples

Each individual material can be of high quality, but together they compete. A limited palette allows you to read the shape of the room more clearly.

Trend without context

A current solution is not an end in itself. It should be compatible with housing, light and people's willingness to use and care for it.

One view thinking

The kitchen is seen from several directions. Sides, transitions, high volumes and how an open work area looks should be evaluated.
