Practical guide

A kitchen that precisely cooperates with the architecture of the room

Built-in kitchen appliances should be designed starting from the fixed points of the room: walls, aisles, windowsills, risers, ventilation and floor levels. The goal is not to mechanically fill every millimeter, but to use the available volume in such a way that cabinets can be opened, appliances can be serviced and the kitchen can be easily maintained.

A well-planned built-in kitchen visually becomes part of the room. The lines of tall cabinets, fillings at the walls and transitions to the ceiling should be evaluated from several points of view. At the same time, you must leave technically necessary gaps and access to places that will need it even after installation.

Installation details

Where does the furniture end and the room begin?

The quality of the installation is most often determined by the transitions: side filling at the wall, closing to the ceiling, plinth at the floor and contact with the windowsill or doorway. These zones are designed deliberately, as they compensate for room deviations and at the same time remain visible.

Access even after installation

Service points for counters, valves, rosettes and appliances must not be hidden behind non-openable construction. The exact solution can be visually sealed while maintaining the required access.

Important nuances

Three mistakes that can weaken the final result

Too close filling

Without side clearances, the door can hit a wall, handle or window sill.

Forget communications access

A closed but unserviceable area may require dismantling of furniture in the future.

Equal access to all niches

For some niches, a cabinet is suitable, for others - filling or a free technical area.

Before consultation

How to prepare useful starting information

Prepare photos of niches, aisles, windowsills and communications, as well as the dimensions of the room at different heights. Specify which equipment is to be built in and which areas must remain accessible. This allows the built-in kitchen solution to be designed precisely, but not at the expense of functionality.
